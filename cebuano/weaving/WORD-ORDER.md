# Cebuano: the order of the words to weave

The commonest English words matched to the Cebuano core words, and from that an order in which to weave them. *Little Redcap* was woven by this order; *The Wolf and the Seven Little Kids* was woven before it and follows it only in part.

The frequencies are from the New General Service List 1.2 (Browne, Culligan and Phillips, CC BY-SA 4.0); see [`weaving/WORD-LISTS.md`](../../weaving/WORD-LISTS.md). The Cebuano words and their tiers are from [`../vocabulary.md`](../vocabulary.md). The decisions already taken for single words are in [`SWAPS.md`](SWAPS.md).

## What the match shows

The 105 commonest English headwords are 56 percent of all the words in ordinary English text. By what can be done with each:

| Kind of swap        | What it means                                                                 | Headwords | Share of all text |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | ----------------- |
| plain               | one Cebuano word, same place in the sentence                                  | 16        | 8.5 %             |
| judgment            | a Cebuano word, but each occurrence has to be looked at                       | 38        | 12.4 %            |
| hides a distinction | *sa*: one word where English chooses between in, at, to                       | 5         | 6.5 %             |
| has to move         | the Cebuano word stands elsewhere in the sentence                             | 7         | 2.3 %             |
| none                | no Cebuano word to put in (the, a, is and was, it, of, will, would ...)       | 39        | 26.1 %            |

So about a fifth of an English text can be woven without touching the sentence, a little over a quarter if *sa* is used, and a quarter of it never can. The shares are upper limits: a "judgment" word is swapped only where it fits, often in fewer than half of its places.

## The order

One order, by how common a word is, counting only the places where it fits.

**Effective frequency** is the frequency of the English word times the share of its places where the Cebuano word could be put. The share is measured on the stories woven so far, and is to be updated with each new one.

Two tales are a small base, so the shares will move. The list counts by headword ("you" with "your", "he" with "him" and "his") while the fit was measured on the forms that were swapped, so the figures for the pronouns are somewhat high. *Sa* is not in the table: its English counterparts (in, to, on, at, into) are among the commonest words of all, and it fitted in 35 places of *Little Redcap*.

Words not yet measured take their place by the rank of their English word in the table of 105 above, until a story has measured them.

## Rules for a story

- **A version with N words takes the first N words of the order that occur in the story,** at least twice in a place where they can be swapped. Where a story lacks a word, the next one takes its place, so each story brings a few words the reader has not met.
- **One harder word in each version, from the first on.** Harder are: one of a pair where Cebuano has two words for one English word (*dili* before *wala*, *ikaw* before *kamo*, *kami* before *kita*); a word that hides a choice English makes (*siya*, *iyang*, *sa*); later, a word that has to move. In *Little Redcap*: *dili* in the first version, *sa* in the second, *wala*, *ayaw* and *iyang* in the third.
- **Sa early.** As the last of 32 words in the first tale the reader was wary of it; as one of 21 in the second it was easy, with English around it and the same places read in English one version before.
- **A story may bring in a rarer core word** when it has it four times or more (*mama*, *bata*, *gikan*), or as a phrase (*Maayong buntag*).
- **Count the places where a word fits, not where the English word stands.** "What" occurs nine times in *Little Redcap* and is a question twice.
- **Prefer words the story explains.** *Adlaw*, day, stood twice in *Little Redcap*, in sentences where many words would fit, and was taken out.

What a level is, in number of words and in how much of the page changes, is not settled; see the log in this folder.
