From the two tales woven so far, *The Wolf and the Seven Little Kids* and *Little Redcap*. The rules behind the decisions are in [`weaving/HOW-TO-WEAVE.md`](../../weaving/HOW-TO-WEAVE.md); the woven stories are in [`../texts-woven/`](../texts-woven/).

## The order they came in

*The Wolf and the Seven Little Kids:*

| Version  | Words | Share of the story                  | Added |
| -------- | ----- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 12 words | 12    | about one word in eight             | ug, siya, mama, unya, sila, gamay, dili, balay, gusto, dako, kabalo, didto |
| 21 words | 21    | a little under one in five          | pero, tanan, nga, ikaw, ako, kami, wala, naa, diri |
| 31 words | 31    | between one in five and one in four | iyang, niingon, adto with its forms, usa, kamo, kita, tubig, inom, tulog, pagkaon |

*Little Redcap,* woven later, by the order of the words in this folder:

| Version  | Words | Share of the story        | Added |
| -------- | ----- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 12 words | 12    | about one in six or seven | ug, siya, ako, nga, ikaw, pero, niingon, unya, niadto, sila, kabalo, dili |
| 21 words | 21    | about one in five         | sa, gamay, diri, balay, dako, bata, didto, usa, gusto |
| 30 words | 30    | about one in four         | iyang, gikan, wala, mama, ayaw, Maayong buntag, unsa, kaon, kung |

A first 12-word version that spent four of its words on *tubig, inom, tulog* and *pagkaon*, once or twice each, was the weaker one: the words to weave first are the ones common in any story.

Words in Tier 1 not woven yet, and why: *kaayo* (comes after its word, so the sentence would have to change); *na, pa, lang, gyud, ba* (their place in the sentence is the point); *ang, og, si, mga* (no English word to stand for); *karon* (the "now" of this tale was not about time).
