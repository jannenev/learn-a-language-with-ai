# Cebuano: the swaps decided so far

For whoever weaves the next story into Cebuano: which English words each Cebuano word has stood for, and where the English was left. From the first tale, *The Wolf and the Seven Little Kids*. The rules behind the decisions are in [`weaving/HOW-TO-WEAVE.md`](../../weaving/HOW-TO-WEAVE.md); the woven stories are in [`../texts-woven/`](../texts-woven/).

Decided with AI assistance; not checked by a native speaker.

## The order they came in

| Version  | Words | Share of the story         | Added |
| -------- | ----- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 12 words | 12    | about one word in eight    | ug, siya, mama, unya, sila, gamay, dili, balay, gusto, dako, kabalo, didto |
| 21 words | 21    | a little under one in five | pero, tanan, nga, ikaw, ako, kami, wala, naa, diri |

Decided as well, and not yet in a published version: *iyang, niingon, adto* with its forms, *usa, kamo, kita, tubig, inom, tulog, pagkaon* and *sa*.

A first 12-word version that spent four of its words on *tubig, inom, tulog* and *pagkaon*, once or twice each, was the weaker one: the words to weave first are the ones common in any story.

Words in Tier 1 not woven yet, and why: *kaayo* (comes after its word, so the sentence would have to change); *na, pa, lang, gyud, ba* (their place in the sentence is the point); *ang, og, si, mga* (no English word to stand for); *karon* (the "now" of this tale was not about time).
