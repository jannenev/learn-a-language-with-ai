# Learn a language with AI: Cebuano (Bisaya) reading texts

Short dialogues in Cebuano, the language of Cebu in the Philippines, called Bisaya by its speakers. The aim is to learn about 120 of the most common words of everyday conversation. By our estimate they are 60 to 70 percent of the words in an ordinary conversation, and 80 to 85 percent in simple exchanges such as greetings and shopping: enough to catch what people are talking about, not yet every sentence. The dialogues are built only from those words, so the words keep coming back.

**How to read.** Read the texts in order. Read the Cebuano first, on its own; then check it against the English, which is word for word, so that you can see which word is which, and not natural English. Come back to each text many times over days and weeks: a few times on the first day, again the next day, then every few days. The first texts take the most reads, ten or more, because every word in them is new. Reading alone may not be enough: it helps to take one sentence at a time, work out what each word does, and learn it by heart. A word is yours when you read it cold without hesitating.

**Status.** These texts were written with the help of an AI assistant and have not yet been checked by a native speaker. Meanings are probably right; stress and naturalness are where errors would hide.
