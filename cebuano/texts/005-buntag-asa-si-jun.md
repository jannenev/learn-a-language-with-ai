# 005 — Buntag: asa si Jun? (Morning: where is Jun?)

The next morning. Mama has no work today and is at home. Ana gets up; Jun's sleeping mat is empty. Out in the yard, people stand at the water tap with buckets.

Words: Tier 1 only. The last four Tier 1 entries first appear: sila, ug, kasabot, and na as "that" (short for kana; until now na was "already / now"). New shapes of known words: kini and kana, the full forms of ni and na; ta'y, ba'y and kinsa'y, more of the 'y family.

How to use: read block 1 on its own first. Then block 2 to check what you understood. Block 3 only for the words you could not place. The English in block 2 stays close to the Cebuano words, so it is not always natural English; where the real meaning differs, block 3 says so.

## 1 · Read (Cebuano only)

Who speaks: Ana speaks from the left margin, Jun indented, Mama indented further. Names are not repeated.

```
(Morning. Mama sits at the table with a cup. Ana comes out of her room.)

Maayong buntag, Ma. Kumusta ka?
                Maayo kaayo, Day. Wala'y trabaho karon!
Asa si Jun? Tulog pa siya?
                Wala ko kabalo. Wala siya diri.

(Ana looks out of the window. In the yard, people stand at the water tap with buckets.)

Ma, naa'y mga tawo didto. Kinsa sila?
                Mga tawo lang. Gusto sila og tubig.
Ug si Jun? Naa siya didto?
                Wala. Wala siya didto.
Dili ni maayo, Ma. Asa siya?

(The door opens. Jun comes in, bent sideways under a big bucket. Water spills on the floor.)

        Maayong buntag! Dali, Ana! Dako kaayo ni!
                Dong! Unsa na?
        Tubig ni, Ma. Daghan!
                Tubig? Dili ko kasabot. Naa ta'y tubig diri.
        Gamay lang ang tubig diri. Imoha kini, Ma.
                Akoa kana? Kasabot na ko. Salamat, Dong!

(Mama takes some coins from her pocket.)

                Naa ko'y kwarta, Dong. Imoha ni.
        Ayaw na, Ma. Dili ko gusto og kwarta.
        Naa ba'y pagkaon? Ang dako…
                Oo, naa pa ang dako. Hulat lang.

(Mama puts the big bundle on the table and opens it. Ana brings three cups.)

Kinsa'y gusto moinom?
        Ako! Ug si Mama.
                Oo. Tubig ug pagkaon. Kaon ta!
Pila ang mga tawo didto, Dong?
        Daghan pa. Naa pa sila didto.
        Lami gyud ni! Unya, matulog ko.
                Salamat, Dong. Maayo kaayo ka.
```

## Listen for

- **They.** *sila*: *Kinsa sila? Gusto sila og tubig. Naa pa sila didto.* With it the persons are complete.
- **And.** *ug*: *Ug si Jun? Tubig ug pagkaon.* It sounds like *og* (some): *gusto sila og tubig* (line 6), *ug si Jun* (line 7).
- **Where is he? / Not here.** *Asa siya?* — *Wala siya diri. Wala siya didto.* The opposite is *naa*: *Naa pa sila didto.*
- **This and that.** *Unsa na?* — *Tubig ni.* (lines 11–12): *na* is that, near you; *ni* is this, near me. The full forms are *kana* and *kini* (lines 14–15).
- **I don't understand.** *Dili ko kasabot.* And when it clears: *Kasabot na ko.*
- **The 'y family grows.** *naa ta'y tubig* (we have water), *naa ba'y pagkaon?* (is there food?), *kinsa'y gusto moinom?* (who wants to drink?).
- **dili, diri, didto.** Not, here, there: lines 4, 8 and 9.
