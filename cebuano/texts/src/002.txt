@ At the table. Next to the plate stands a small bowl with a lid on.
Ana | Lami ni, Dong. | Tasty, this, Dong.
    Lami=tasty ni,=this Dong.=to-a-boy
    ! Dong = how you address a boy or a younger brother (short for Dodong)
    ! ni = this (short for kini); it comes last: lami ni = this is tasty
Jun | Unsa ni? | What's this?
    Unsa=what ni?=this
Ana | Wala ko kabalo. Kaon lang. | I don't know. Just eat.
    Wala=not ko=I kabalo.=know Kaon=eat lang.=just
    ! wala ko kabalo, literally "not I know", is the everyday "I don't know"
    ! lang = just, only; it comes after the word it belongs to
@ Jun lifts the lid and tastes.
Jun | Lami gyud ni! Daghan kaayo! | Really tasty, this! Very much!
    Lami=tasty gyud=really ni!=this Daghan=much kaayo!=very
    ! gyud = really, for sure; it comes after the word it strengthens, like kaayo
Ana | Oo, daghan gyud. Daghan ang pagkaon. | Yes, really much. Much, the food.
    Oo,=yes daghan=much gyud.=really Daghan=much ang=the(subj) pagkaon.=food
    ! ang marks the subject, what the sentence is about; "the food is much" = there's a lot of food
Jun | Nikaon na si Mama? | Has Mama eaten already?
    Nikaon=ate na=already si=[name] Mama?
    ! si marks a person's name as the subject; it has no English equivalent
Ana | Oo. | Yes.
    Oo.=yes
Jun | Asa si Mama? Naa sa balay? | Where is Mama? Is in the house?
    Asa=where si=[name] Mama? Naa=is-there sa=in balay?=house
Ana | Wala sa balay. Naa sa trabaho. | Not in the house. Is at work.
    Wala=not-there sa=in balay.=house Naa=is-there sa=at trabaho.=work
    ! no "she": the subject is left out when it is obvious from the question
    ! sa = in, at, to: the one word for place. wala = is not there, naa = is there; the same words as wala (not) and naa'y (there is) in text 001
Jun | Sa trabaho? Adto ta! | At work? Let's go!
    Sa=at trabaho?=work Adto=go ta!=we(let's)
    ! root verb + ta = "let's": Adto ta! like Kaon ta!
Ana | Karon? | Now?
    Karon?=now
Jun | Oo, karon! | Yes, now!
    Oo,=yes karon!=now
Ana | Unya lang. Kaon ta karon. Adto ta unya. | Just later. Let's eat now. Let's go later.
    Unya=later lang.=just Kaon=eat ta=we(let's) karon.=now Adto=go ta=we(let's) unya.=later
Ana | Moadto ko didto unya. | I'll go there later.
    Moadto=will-go ko=I didto=there unya.=later
    ! mo- = will, as in mokaon in text 001: moadto = will go. didto = there, far from both of us
Jun | Moadto pud ko didto. Naa ko'y kwarta. | I'll go there too. I have money.
    Moadto=will-go pud=also ko=I didto.=there Naa=there-is ko'y=I kwarta.=money
    ! naa ko'y kwarta = literally "there is, I, money" = I have money
Ana | Naa pud ko'y kwarta. Sige, Dong. | I have money too. OK, Dong.
    Naa=there-is pud=also ko'y=I kwarta.=money Sige,=okay Dong.=to-a-boy

## Listen for

- **What's this? / I don't know.** *Unsa ni?* — *Wala ko kabalo.*
- **Where is … ? / Is at …** *Asa si Mama?* — *Wala sa balay. Naa sa trabaho.* The pair *wala* (not there) and *naa* (there), and *sa* for the place.
- **Now and later.** *Kaon ta karon. Adto ta unya.* One-word answers work too: *Karon?* — *Unya.*
- **Let's go / I'll go.** *Adto ta!* is built like *Kaon ta!* in text 001; *mo-* in front makes it "will": *Moadto ko*.
- **There is / I have.** *Naa ko'y kwarta* (I have money), built like *Naa'y pagkaon* in text 001.
