    ! maayong = maayo + the linker -ng, which glues a describing word to its noun
Ana | Maayong buntag, Jun. Kumusta ka? | Good morning, Jun. How are you?
    Maayong=good buntag,=morning Jun. Kumusta=how-are ka?=you
Jun | Maayo, salamat. Ikaw? | Good, thanks. You?
    Maayo,=good salamat.=thanks Ikaw?=you
    ! ikaw = the full form of "you", used on its own; inside a sentence it is ka
Ana | Maayo pud. Nikaon na ka? | Good too. Have you eaten already?
    Maayo=good pud.=also Nikaon=ate na=already ka?=you
    ! ni- = did, na = already. "Nikaon na ka?" is the everyday greeting right after hello.
Jun | Wala pa. Gusto ko mokaon karon. | Not yet. I want to eat now.
    Wala=not pa.=yet Gusto=want ko=I mokaon=will-eat karon.=now
    ! wala = not / none, for things that did not happen; mo- = will
Ana | Naa'y pagkaon sa balay. Kaon ta! | There is food in the house. Let's eat!
    Naa'y=there-is pagkaon=food sa=in balay.=house Kaon=eat ta!=we(let's)
    ! naa'y = naa + 'y (a squeezed ang): "there is a ...". Root verb + ta = "let's".
Jun | Sige! Lami ba? | OK! Tasty?
    Sige!=okay Lami=tasty ba?=[?]
    ! ba turns the sentence into a yes/no question
Ana | Oo, lami kaayo. | Yes, very tasty.
    Oo,=yes lami=tasty kaayo.=very
Jun | Daghan kaayo ang pagkaon! | Very much, the food!
    Daghan=much kaayo=very ang=the(subj) pagkaon!=food
    ! ang marks the subject, what the sentence is about; "the food is very much" = there's a lot of food
Jun | Unsa ni? | What's this?
    Unsa=what ni?=this
Ana | Wala ko kabalo. Kaon lang. | I don't know. Just eat.
    Wala=not ko=I kabalo.=know Kaon=eat lang.=just
    ! wala ko kabalo, literally "not I know", is the everyday "I don't know"
Jun | Lami gyud ni! | Really tasty, this!
    Lami=tasty gyud=really ni!=this
Jun | Asa si Mama? | Where is Mama?
    Asa=where si=[name] Mama?
    ! si marks a person's name as the subject; it has no English equivalent
Ana | Naa sa trabaho. Moadto ko didto unya. | Is at work. I'll go there later.
    Naa=is-there sa=at trabaho.=work Moadto=will-go ko=I didto=there unya.=later
    ! no "she": the subject is left out when it is obvious from the question
Jun | Moadto pud ko. Naa ko'y kwarta. | I'll go too. I have money.
    Moadto=will-go pud=also ko.=I Naa=there-is ko'y=I kwarta.=money
    ! naa ko'y kwarta = literally "there is, I, money" = I have money (naa + ko + 'y)

    Sige.=okay Kaon=eat ta=we(let's) karon,=now adto=go ta=we(let's) unya.=later

    Sige,=okay Dong.=to-a-boy
    ! Dong = how you address a boy or a younger brother (short for Dodong); sige here = "sure, alright"

## Listen for

- **Have you … ? / Not yet.** *Nikaon na ka?* — *Wala pa.* The pair *na* (already) and *pa* (yet) does the work.
- **There is / I have.** *Naa'y pagkaon* (there is food), *Naa ko'y kwarta* (I have money). Both are *naa* = there-is.
- **Let's …** → root + *ta*: *Kaon ta! Adto ta!*
- **Future vs past prefix.** *mo-kaon* (will eat) vs *ni-kaon* (ate); *mo-adto* (will go).
- **very** → adjective + *kaayo*: *lami kaayo, daghan kaayo*.
