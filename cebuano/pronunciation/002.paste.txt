Read the Cebuano (Bisaya) text below aloud. You do not need to know the language. Apply these rules exactly and do not anglicise anything.
- Pronounce it like Filipino (Tagalog), never like English.
- Vowels are pure and never reduced: a as in "father", e as in "bet", i as in "machine", o as in "or", u as in "food". Every written vowel is its own syllable.
- g is always hard as in "go". j is as in "jam". y as in "yes". r is a single tap as in Spanish. ng is one sound as in "sing", also at the start of a word (nga, ngano). mga is said "ma-NGA".
- A hyphen inside a word is a glottal stop, a catch in the throat as in "uh-oh": bag-o, kan-on, tan-aw.
- An acute accent marks the stressed syllable: salámat, kumustá, maáyo, túbig, baláy. A grave accent on a final vowel means the word ends in a glottal stop: lamì, dílì. A circumflex means a stressed final syllable that ends in a glottal stop: dakô, walâ. Words without a mark are unstressed little words.
- Read each line at a calm natural speed, pause briefly between lines, and do not translate.

Lamì ni, Dong.
Únsa ni?
Walâ ko kabálo. Káon lang.
Lamì gyud ni! Dághan kaáyo!
Óo, dághan gyud. Dághan ang pagkáon.
Nikáon na si Máma?
Óo.
Ása si Máma? Náa sa baláy?
Walâ sa baláy. Náa sa trabáho.
Sa trabáho? Ádto ta!
Karón?
Óo, karón!
Únya lang. Káon ta karón. Ádto ta únya.
Moádto ko didtó únya.
Moádto pud ko didtó. Náa ko'y kwárta.
Náa pud ko'y kwárta. Síge, Dong.
