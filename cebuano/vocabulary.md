# Core vocabulary — Cebuano / Bisaya

Curated by category for *listening comprehension*. Each entry gives the standard spelling, the forms you actually hear or see in texting, the meaning, and one short example. Verbs are given as a root plus the common heard forms (see `grammar-notes.md` for what the prefixes mean).

Counts are approximate; an "entry" is one word or a tight cluster (e.g. the three greetings, or numbers 1–10).

## Conventions

- **Spelling:** standard modern Cebuano orthography, with the spoken/texting form given alongside where it differs: *gyud* (also *jud*), *dili* / *di*, *wala* / *wa*, *kini* / *ni*, *kana* / *na*, *kahibalo* / *kabalo*.
- **Hyphen = glottal stop** (a small catch in the throat): *bag-o* (new), *kan-on* (rice), *tan-aw* (look), *kanus-a* (when).
- **Verbs are listed by root**, with the forms you will actually hear: *kaon* → *mokaon* (will eat), *nikaon* (ate), *nagkaon* (is eating), *gikaon* (was eaten).
- **Vowels** are pure: a, e, i, o, u as in Spanish or Finnish. In speech *o/u* and *e/i* swap freely (*babaye* ~ *babayi*, *tulo* ~ *tulu*).
- **Pronouns**: the short forms (*ko, ka, ta, mi, mo*) are what you hear; full forms (*ako, ikaw, kita, kami, kamo*) appear at the start of a sentence or for emphasis.

## Tier 1 — Survival core (65 entries)

Goal: follow a simple dialogue at home or in a shop. Texts 001–006 use only these.

### 1.1 Pronouns (7)

The short form is what you hear inside a sentence. The "my / me" column is used both for possession (*akong balay* = my house) and for "me/by me" after verbs (*gikaon nako* = I ate it / eaten by me).

| Full | Short | my / me | mine | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ako | ko | nako / akong | akoa | I, me |
| ikaw | ka | nimo / imong | imoha | you (one person) |
| siya | — | niya / iyang | iyaha | he, she (no gender in Cebuano) |
| kita | ta | nato / atong | atoa | we (including you) — *Kaon ta!* Let's eat! |
| kami | mi | namo / among | amoa | we (not you) |
| kamo | mo | ninyo / inyong | inyoha | you (several people) |
| sila | — | nila / ilang | ilaha | they |

Watch out: *ko* is both "I" and "my/me" in speech: *Gusto ko* (I want), *balay nako / akong balay* (my house). And *kita* also means "see" (*nakita* = saw); context decides.

### 1.2 Markers — the glue words (8)

| Word | Also | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ang | — | the; marks the subject | *Lami ang pagkaon.* — The food is delicious. |
| si | — | marks a person's name as subject | *Asa si Ana?* — Where is Ana? |
| sa | — | in / at / to / on / of — very general | *Naa siya sa balay.* — She's at home. |
| og | ug | a / some; marks an indefinite object | *Gusto ko og tubig.* — I want (some) water. |
| ug | og | and | *Ana ug Jun* — Ana and Jun |
| nga | -ng | linker: "that / which"; joins a describer to its noun | *maayong buntag* = *maayo nga buntag* — good morning |
| mga | — | plural marker (said "manga") | *mga tawo* — people |
| ni | — | of (before a name) | *balay ni Ana* — Ana's house |

Notes: *og* and *ug* sound the same; texting spells both any which way. *ni* is also the short form of *kini* "this" (*Unsa ni?* — What's this?).

### 1.3 Particles — they carry the mood (7)

You will hear these in almost every sentence, clustered right after the first word.

| Word | Also | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ba | — | yes/no question marker | *Lami ba?* — Is it tasty? |
| na | — | already / now / anymore | *Nikaon na ko.* — I've already eaten. · *Kaon na!* — Eat now! · *Dili na.* — Not anymore. |
| pa | — | still / yet / more | *Wala pa.* — Not yet. · *Naa pa?* — Is there still some? |
| lang | ra, lamang | only / just | *Ako lang.* — Just me. · *Gamay lang.* — Just a little. |
| gyud | jud, gyod, gayod | really / definitely | *Lami gyud!* — It's really good! |
| kaayo | — | very (comes *after* the adjective) | *Dako kaayo.* — Very big. |
| pud | sad, usab | also / too | *Ako pud.* — Me too. |

*na* is one word: the situation has changed, the new state is here. English picks "already" after a finished action, "now" with a command or plan, "anymore" after a no. *na* is also short for *kana* "that" (*Unsa na?* — What's that?). Context decides.

### 1.4 Yes / no / there is (4)

| Word | Also | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| oo | o | yes | *Oo, lami.* — Yes, it's tasty. |
| dili | di | no / not — before future verbs, adjectives, nouns | *Dili ko moadto.* — I won't go. · *Dili lami.* — Not tasty. |
| wala | wa | no / none / didn't; there isn't — before past verbs and for absence | *Wala ko nikaon.* — I didn't eat. · *Wala'y tubig.* — There's no water. |
| naa | aduna, duna | there is / have | *Naa ko'y kwarta.* — I have money. · *Naa siya sa balay.* — He's at home. |

Contractions you will hear constantly: *naa'y / naay* (= naa + ang: there is a …), *wala'y / walay* (there is no …), *ko'y / koy* (= ko + 'y: *Naa koy kwarta*).

### 1.5 Question words (4)

| Word | Also | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| unsa | unsa'y, unsay (= unsa ang) | what | *Unsa ni?* — What's this? · *Unsay imong ngalan?* — What's your name? |
| kinsa | kinsa'y | who | *Kinsa siya?* — Who is he? |
| asa | hain, diin | where | *Asa ka?* — Where are you (going)? · *Asa si Mama?* — Where's Mom? |
| pila | tagpila | how many / how much | *Pila ni?* — How much is this? |

Note on *asa*: strictly *asa* = where to, *hain* = where is (a thing), *diin* = where from / where did. In Cebu speech *asa* is used for all three; *Diin ka gikan?* (Where did you come from?) is the one fixed phrase with *diin* you will hear.

### 2.8 Numbers (1 cluster)

Native Cebuano numbers are used for counting things and small quantities; **Spanish-derived numbers are used for money, time and prices**, so in a shop you will mostly hear the Spanish ones.

| # | Cebuano | Spanish-derived (money / time) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | usa | uno |
| 2 | duha | dos |
| 3 | tulo | tres |
| 4 | upat | kwatro |
| 5 | lima | singko |
| 6 | unom | says (seis) |
| 7 | pito | syete |
| 8 | walo | otso |
| 9 | siyam | nwebe |
| 10 | napulo | dyes |
| 20 | — | baynte |
| 50 | — | singkwenta |
| 100 | usa ka gatos | syen / syento |

*Pila ni?* — *Baynte pesos.* · *Tulo ka bata* — three children (note *ka* between number and noun) · *alas dos* — two o'clock.
